# Japanese Learning — Notes & Realisations

## Why most people fail
- Traditional study ≠ fluency
- You can study forever without touching real Japanese
- Immersion is non-negotiable

## Core idea I’m following
- Language is learned through **pattern exposure**, not rules
- Grammar/vocab help, but **input is king**
- You only get better by engaging with stuff you barely understand

## Important mindset shifts
- I will suck for a long time and that’s normal
- Waiting to feel “ready” is a trap
- Beginner resources are training wheels, not the road

## Immersion ≠ studying
- Immersion = native content for natives
- Anime, YouTube, games, novels
- Not pausing every second, especially while listening

## Kana vs Kanji (what actually matters)
- Kana: finite, grind once, move on
- Kanji: only make sense **inside words**
- Never study kanji in isolation unless I’m confused

## Vocabulary strategy
- Learn words, not kanji
- Anki is just memory support, not learning itself
- Mining later > premade decks forever

## Grammar
- Grammar is a reference, not something to master upfront
- Learn just enough to notice it in immersion

## Output (speaking/writing)
- Delaying output is unnecessary
- Output doesn’t make me fluent, but it reinforces input
- Perfectionism here is harmful
